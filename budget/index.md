Vendor pricing is commercially confidential and is not published here. The
structure is the part that transfers to another institution anyway.

## Three prices, not one

Budgeting for this kind of facility goes wrong when it is treated as a single
capital purchase. There are three distinct costs:

**Third-party instrumentation.** One-time. The instruments themselves, bought
from their own vendors. This is the number people quote, and it is well under
half of the first year.

**Enclosures and integration.** One-time, charged per cart. Each cart is a
micro-environment with its own transport and arm, so this scales linearly with
how many instruments you want running *under* automation rather than beside it.

**Software and support subscription.** Annual, charged per cart, starting in
year one. This recurs forever and decides whether the facility is affordable in
year five.

In our case the recurring subscription alone is a substantial fraction of the
instrument spend every year, and the enclosures cost roughly half again what the
instruments do. **A realistic first-year total is close to double what an
instrument list suggests.**

## What is usually excluded

Factory acceptance testing, shipping, installation and site acceptance testing
were all excluded from the proposal we received, as was any movement in vendor
pricing between proposal and order. Budget for every one of them.

## Support tiers

Expect a choice between a lower tier that bills protocol development by the hour
and a higher tier that includes it. Do the arithmetic carefully: the premium
between tiers can buy far more hours of protocol writing than a realistic
protocol set will ever consume.

The deciding factor is usually elsewhere. If the lower tier excludes support for
third-party instruments, and nearly every instrument is third-party, then the
tier choice is really a question about **who fixes the instruments**, not who
writes the protocols.

## Lab space

Not in an instrument quote. A fifteen-cart system needs floor area, power and
compressed air, and that is a separate negotiation with your institution.

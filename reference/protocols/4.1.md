## Resource availability

**Lead contact.** The foundry's lead. No personal address is recorded in
this repository by convention; a role address will be used if one is created.

**Materials availability.** Gene fragments are ordered per design and are
not a shared resource. The 384-well template plate produced here is held in
ambient random-access storage and is the input to 4.3, 4.4 and 4.13.

**Data and code availability.** Transfer lists and per-well delivered
volumes are instrument output. No analysis code is specific to this
protocol.

## Experimental model and subject details

Not applicable. No cells, no organism, no cell line. That is the point of
this protocol — the clonal array is built from synthetic linear dsDNA and
never passes through a host.

## Method details

**Scale.** 384 designs per plate, one hour, one ordered gene fragment per
design delivered plated.

1. **Resuspend the ordered fragments.** Add nuclease-free water to each well
   at the vendor's stated concentration — eBlocks at 10 ng/µL, Twist
   fragments at 0.5 ng/µL. Done by hand, off the deck: the fragments arrive
   plated, so this is one addition per plate rather than one per design.
2. **Normalise every design to one concentration.** One acoustic transfer at
   a fixed mass per well, backfilled with water. 384 wells in about three
   minutes. The normalisation is **by transferred mass, not by volume**,
   which is the whole reason this step is on the Echo: the bench alternative
   quantifies each well, computes a dilution and pipettes it, and the
   resulting molar ratio drifts design to design.
3. **Keep the source plate.** The plate returns to ambient random-access
   storage and is retrieved when 4.3, 4.4 or 4.13 ask for it.

**Why the Bravo never appears.** No dispensing step here uses the
96-channel head, which is why 384 format costs nothing in this protocol and
costs roughly 4× everywhere the Bravo does the dispensing.

## Quantification and statistical analysis

There is no statistical analysis. The quantitative claim is that every well
of the destination plate carries the same mass of DNA, and it rests on the
acoustic dispenser's per-transfer volume control rather than on a
measurement made afterwards. No plate-level QC step is specified in the
repository for this protocol — if a concentration check is ever wanted, it
would be the dsDNA-dye-plus-standard-curve read that CF2H uses.

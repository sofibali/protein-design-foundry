# STAR Methods — 4.14 Heat-shock transformation

_Working document, 2026-11-23. Derived from `_data/protocols.yml`
(`workflows:`, id `4.14`) and from the citation audit in
`00-citation-audit.md`. Intended practice; the facility installs April 2027._

**Citation status: standard practice with a canonical origin.** Chemical
transformation with a ~45 s pulse at 42 °C is Hanahan, *J Mol Biol* 1983,
[10.1016/s0022-2836(83)80284-8](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0022-2836(83)80284-8)
— "Studies on transformation of *Escherichia coli* with plasmids" (resolved
live 2026-11-23). It is textbook, and citing it is optional in a way that
citing Studier for ZYM-5052 in 4.4 is not. **What is genuinely this
facility's is the implementation**, not the biology: two
temperature-controlled deck nests at 4 °C and 42 °C with the pulse as a
plate move between them.

## Resource availability

**Lead contact.** As 4.1.

**Materials availability.** Transformants in liquid — a pool, or one well
per design — plus a titre from the plated dilution series. **Nothing here
picks a colony**, so no step returns a clone from agar back to liquid, and
no clonal isolate is produced for distribution.

**Data and code availability.** The titre from the dilution series is the
protocol's quantitative output. No analysis code is specific to it.

## Experimental model and subject details

*E. coli*, chemically competent, transformed in 96-well plate format. The
cells are held cold throughout except for the pulse itself. No strain is
named in the workflow entry; 4.4, which consumes the transformants, uses
BL21(DE3).

## Method details

**Scale.** 96 per plate, ~2 h plus an hour of recovery.

1. **Dispense DNA onto a cold plate.** Acoustic dispense into a plate already
   sitting on the 4 °C deck nest.
2. **Add competent cells, cold.** A 96-channel addition with the plate never
   leaving 4 °C. ~8 min at 96.
3. **The 45-second pulse.** A plate move from the 4 °C nest to the 42 °C nest
   and back. **This is the only way the deck can time it**: a single
   temperature block ramps at about 12 K/min and would take roughly three
   minutes to cross from 4 °C to 42 °C, and a thermal cycler in another cart
   cannot time a 45 s pulse at all. Two fixed-temperature positions, one
   move.
4. **Recover.** Recovery medium bulk-dispensed immediately after the pulse,
   then shaking on the deck for about an hour.
5. **Outgrow** in a shaking incubator position, scheduled with the rest of
   the day.
6. **Titre, QC and sequence.** Plates are lidded on deck; the dilution series
   is for counting and checking only. Colony picking is out of scope on the
   rack, and sequencing is off the deck.

## Quantification and statistical analysis

**Titre**, from the plated dilution series, by colony count. No target
titre, dilution factors or plating volume are stated in
`_data/protocols.yml`, and no gate is attached to this workflow.

The protocol's own boundary is the thing worth restating: transformation and
plating are in scope on the rack, colony picking is not. That is why plating
exists here for titre and QC rather than as a route to a clone, and it is
the constraint that makes 4.13 — assembly straight into expression with no
cells at all — the preferred arrayed route.

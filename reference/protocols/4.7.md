## Experimental model and subject details

Not applicable — all four formats are homogeneous in-vitro assays. The
NanoBiT arm runs on crude cell-free reaction mixture rather than on purified
protein, which is its main advantage and the reason it sits first in the
ladder.

## Method details

**Scale.** 384 routine, 1536 reachable. 1–2 h.

1. **Pick the format.** The choice is the same at either scale. NanoBiT reads
   out of crude cell-free and needs no purification at all; HTRF,
   AlphaScreen and FP each need protein in hand.
2. **Build the plate.** Acoustic, so protein, target and controls land at 384
   or 1536 for the same ~3 min it would cost at 96.
3. **Develop and seal.** Bulk dispense, then a thermal seal.
4. **Hold to equilibrium** in ambient random-access storage, returned when
   the reader is free.
5. **One read.** ~8 min for a TR-FRET plate, ~3 min if the format is
   luminescent.

**Every format here is tipless on the way in and one read on the way out.**
That is why this is the cheapest thing the foundry does per design, and why
the ceiling that matters is the reader's ~150 plates in a 20 h day rather
than the liquid handler's ~75 two-plate protocols.

**Stated limitation.** All four formats need protein in hand except
NanoBiT — and that constraint is what CF2H exists to remove.

## Quantification and statistical analysis

The reported quantity depends on the format: a **TR-FRET ratio** (665/620)
for HTRF, a **luminescence count** for NanoBiT and AlphaScreen, a
**polarisation** value for FP. One read per plate, at equilibrium.

No pass/fail threshold is stated for 4.7 itself in `_data/protocols.yml` —
the workflow carries gates **D3** and **D4**, whose criteria live in
`_data/gates.yml`. The one published threshold convention recorded anywhere
in this repository for a binding call is CF2H's (robust z ≥ 3.0 **and**
fold-activation ≥ 2.0 in ≥2 of 3 replicates), and the argument there — that
a fixed fold cutoff ignores how noisy the plate was, so it has to be paired
with a noise-aware statistic — applies equally to any single-point format
run here.

**Two format-specific cautions**, both documented in the sources above:
AlphaScreen shows a hook effect at high analyte concentration, so a strong
binder can read as a weak one; FP requires roughly a 10× mass ratio between
tracer and binding partner, which rules it out for protein-protein pairs.

# STAR Methods — 4.8 Counter-screen and competition

_Working document, 2026-11-23. Derived from `_data/protocols.yml`
(`workflows:`, id `4.8`) and from the citation audit in
`00-citation-audit.md`. Intended practice; the facility installs April 2027._

**Citation status: standard practice with a canonical origin, plus one
published result the repository relies on and does not cite here.** Using a
point mutant at a designed interface residue to ask whether a binder found
the face it was designed for is alanine-scanning logic: Cunningham & Wells,
*Science* 1989,
[10.1126/science.2471267](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.2471267) (resolved
live 2026-11-23). The specific claim that Δsignal on a hotspot mutant tracks
ΔΔG and ranks mutants correctly by affinity is from Capin et al., *Nat
Commun* 2026 ([10.1038/s41467-026-69741-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-69741-1)),
where it was shown against SPR — stated in `protocols/bind.cf2h.screen.md`
and not attached to 4.8, which is where a reader of the workflows page meets
the idea.

## Method details

**Scale.** The same plate as 4.7 — this is three extra columns, which is why
the counter-screen costs almost nothing new.

1. **Lay three control columns onto the binding plate**, acoustically, onto
   the plate 4.7 already built: the counter-target homolog, a hotspot
   point-mutant of your own target, and a non-binder control from an
   unrelated campaign.
2. **Add a competitor titration** where an IC50 is wanted, built
   acoustically into the same destination plate from one titrant source.
3. **Develop, seal and equilibrate** as 4.7, on the same plate.
4. **Read in one pass.** The bench alternative is two reads, because the
   controls sit on their own plate.
5. **Report the selectivity margin**: on-target signal against each control,
   per design.

## Quantification and statistical analysis

Two quantities: a **selectivity margin** (on-target signal against each of
the three controls, per design) and, where a titration was included, an
**IC50** from the fitted competition curve.

**Gate D6** — did it bind the right face? — is decided here. The structure
of the answer is what the protocol is for: a homolog counter-target
establishes selectivity against a related surface, but only the hotspot
point-mutant of the designed target distinguishes a binder that engaged the
designed interface from one that found some other face of the right protein.
A design whose signal is unmoved by mutating its own designed hotspots did
not bind where it was designed to.

No numeric threshold for the margin is stated in `_data/protocols.yml`; the
gate's criteria live in `_data/gates.yml`.
